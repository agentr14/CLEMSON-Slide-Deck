# CLEMSON-Slide-Deck
Week 8 Final Project Check-In
1. Summary of Accomplishments
This week was all about bringing everything together into the final slide deck and tightening up the full project. I finished organizing my research question, methods, results, and conclusions into a clean, easy-to-follow presentation. I also refined several sections of the written content so the wording flows better and aligns with earlier professor feedback. Most of the heavy lifting was formatting and polishing kind of like that last pass on a DoD test report before it’s allowed anywhere near a review board. I used ChatGPT, Google Slides, and a few APA formatting tools to make sure everything looks professional and consistent.

2. Challenges and Issues
My biggest challenge this week was making the final deck feel cohesive without rewriting half the project. Every time I cleaned up a slide, I found something else to tighten in the wording, which could’ve turned into a time sink if I let it. I also had to double-check that every reference was still accurate after editing the flow of the content. To keep myself from going overboard, I set a “one revision pass per section” rule and stuck to it. At this point, no major issues remain.

3. Plans for Next Week
Next week is really just submission mode. My goal is to do one last proofread, make sure the slides match the written piece perfectly, and confirm APA formatting end-to-end. I also want to check for any accidental formatting glitches in the slide deck (fonts love to misbehave at the last minute). I don’t anticipate needing any major resources just time for a final quality check.

4. Reflection and Learnings
This last stretch taught me how much the structure of a project impacts how clearly the work comes across. Pulling everything into a slide deck forced me to streamline my ideas and focus on what actually matters. I also learned that polishing takes almost as much discipline as building once you start refining, it’s tempting to keep tweaking forever. Overall, the process helped me get better at balancing detail with clarity, and that’s something I’ll carry into future projects.
